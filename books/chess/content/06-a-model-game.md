# 6. Greet and Arkell: pressure on the centre

Andrew Greet has White; Keith Arkell has Black. You are looking at this game
from Black's side, so Greet's name sits above the board and Arkell's below it.
The names stay in view as you read the commentary. If a name is shortened,

With :move[3. e5], White gains space and fixes the central pawn structure.
Black's :move[3... c5] immediately challenges its base on d4. As you step
through the opening, notice how the pressure on the centre leads to exchanges
rather than a quiet build-up behind the pawns.

The game becomes sharp after :move[9... g5]. Follow the pawn captures on the
kingside: opening lines can be more important than keeping every pawn.

By :move[12... Qh4+], Black's queen is joining the attack. White's king has to
move, and Black's minor pieces soon find checking squares. Try to identify
those squares before playing the next move.

The final :move[18... Nf3+] wins with a knight fork: the knight checks the
king on e1 and attacks the queen on d2. Black wins the game.
