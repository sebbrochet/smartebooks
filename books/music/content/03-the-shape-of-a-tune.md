# 3. The shape of a whole tune

Chapter two was one phrase. Most tunes are several, and they are rarely all
different — a melody you have known since childhood is usually two or three
ideas, arranged so that returning to the first one feels like coming home.

:::music-piece{src="assets/twinkle.abc" caption="Ah! vous dirai-je, maman — better known with other words"}

Twelve bars, and only two ideas in them.

The first four bars are the tune everybody knows: it climbs to :note[A], turns
at :note[G]{nth=3}, and walks back down to :note[C]{nth=3}. Call that **A**.

The middle four bars are different. They start lower, on :note[G]{nth=5}, and
do the same small descending step twice — a gentler idea that does not resolve,
which is why it feels like the middle of something. Call that **B**.

Then **A** returns, note for note, and the tune ends where it began.

So the whole thing is **A B A** — and once you hear that, you have heard the
shape of an enormous amount of music. Press play and listen for the moment the
first idea comes back.

:::

## Why tunes repeat themselves

A melody has to be remembered while it is still being heard. Repetition is how
that is done: the second time an idea arrives you recognise it, and recognising
it is most of what enjoying a tune consists of.

> 📌 **Key concept**: when you find yourself expecting the next note, the tune
> has taught you its shape — and it taught you by repeating itself.
