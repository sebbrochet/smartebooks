# 1. The stave

Written music looks like a code, and people assume it is one — that somewhere
there is a key telling you that *this* squiggle means *that* sound. There isn't.
It is a picture of what the music does, and once you have seen what the picture
is a picture *of*, most of it reads itself.

> 📌 **Key concept**: the page is a graph. Up the page is higher in pitch, along
> the page is later in time. Almost everything else is detail.

## Five lines, and why

A note needs somewhere to sit, and "a bit higher than that one" is not precise
enough to play from. So the page gives it five lines to sit on or between,
which is enough room for most singing voices without the eye losing count.

:::music-figure{caption="A note on the bottom line, then one in the space above it"}

```abc
X:1
K:C
E2 F2|
```

:::

The first note is lower than the second, and you can see that it is lower
because it is drawn lower. That is the whole of pitch notation. Everything
else — the extra symbols, the little lines above and below — exists to let the
same trick keep working when the music goes beyond the five lines.

## Higher and lower, in steps

Move up one line or one space at a time and you are walking up the alphabet:
A, B, C, D, E, F, G, and then A again, because after seven letters the pattern
starts over at a pitch that sounds like the first one over again.

:::music-figure{caption="Eight notes in a row — the letters run out and start again"}

```abc
X:1
K:C
CDEF|GABc|
```

:::

Play that on anything and you have the sound everyone recognises as a scale.
The last note has the same letter as the first, and the two sound so alike that
almost every musical culture treats them as the same note.

> 💡 **Tip**: the lowest note above is written on a short line of its own below
> the stave. That is not a different kind of note — it is the fifth line's
> pattern continuing for one note, because the music went below the room it had.

## How long a note lasts

Pitch is where the note sits. Length is what the note is *made of*: an empty
oval lasts longer than a filled one, and a filled one with a tail is shorter
still.

:::music-figure{caption="The same pitch, held for different lengths"}

```abc
X:1
K:C
C4|C2C2|CCCC|
```

:::

Three bars, each lasting the same amount of time. The first spends it on one
note, the second on two, the third on four. The vertical lines are bar lines,
and they divide the music into equal measures of time — which is how you can
follow a piece without counting every note.

> ⚠️ **Pitfall**: an empty note-head does *not* mean silence. It means a longer
> note. Silence is written with its own symbols, and they are the one part of
> notation that really does have to be learned by name.
