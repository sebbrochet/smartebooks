# The Cellar Door

Twenty sections. You begin at 1, and the contents list beside you starts one entry long — it grows as
you read, because in a book like this the table of contents is your own history rather than a map of
somewhere you have not been.

## 1

The cellar door stands open at the bottom of the garden, and the lamp in the kitchen window has gone
out. You did not leave it open.

If you go down at once, :choice{to="2"}.

If you fetch the lantern from the shed first, :choice{to="3"}.

## 2

The steps are wet. Four down, the dark closes over you like water, and your hand finds a wall that is
not where the wall should be.

If you feel your way along it, :choice{to="4"}.

If you go back up while you still know the way, :choice{to="1"}.

## 3

The shed smells of creosote and last summer. The lantern is where it always is, and beside it, which
is not where it always is, a key on a loop of green string.

If you take the key as well, :choice{to="5"}.

If you leave it — it is not yours — :choice{to="4"}.

## 4

The wall gives way to a doorframe, and the doorframe to a room that your house does not have. A
single chair faces the corner, as though someone has been made to sit in it.

If you sit down, :choice{to="6"}.

If you turn the chair around, :choice{to="7"}.

## 5

With the lantern lit the cellar is only a cellar: shelves, a bicycle, jars of something that was
plums. The green key fits a door behind the shelves that you have somehow never once noticed.

If you open it, :choice{to="8"}.

If you put the key back and go to bed, :choice{to="9"}.

## 6

You sit. The corner is very close. After a while you hear the cellar door shut at the top of the
steps, and after a longer while you stop minding.

*Your story ends here.*

## 7

The chair scrapes. Facing the room, it is an ordinary chair, and the room is an ordinary room with a
low ceiling and a smell of coal — and a door in the far wall with a green-painted frame.

If you go through, :choice{to="8"}.

If you carry the chair back up to the kitchen, because it is yours now, :choice{to="10"}.

## 8

Behind the green door is a staircase going up, which is wrong, because you came down. At the top is a
kitchen with the lamp lit in the window.

If you call out, :choice{to="11"}.

If you go quietly to the window, :choice{to="12"}.

## 9

You sleep badly and wake to find the cellar door shut and bolted from the inside, the bolt on your
side, your own hand still on it.

*Your story ends here.*

## 10

You put the chair by the kitchen table, and it fits as though it has always been the fourth one. In
the morning there are four cups on the draining board.

*Your story ends here.*

## 11

"You took your time," says your own voice from the hall, and you hear yourself put the kettle on.

If you answer, :choice{to="13"}.

If you go back down the green stairs, :choice{to="14"}.

## 12

Through the glass you can see the garden, the shed, and the cellar door standing open at the bottom
of it. A figure is going down the steps with a lantern. It has your walk.

If you knock on the glass, :choice{to="15"}.

If you blow out the lamp, :choice{to="16"}.

## 13

The two of you sit at the table until it gets light, and neither of you asks the question. It is
company, of a kind.

*Your story ends here.*

## 14

The green stairs go down a long way further than they went up. You are still descending.

*Your story ends here.*

## 15

The figure stops. It does not look up, because it knows exactly which window you are at, and it lifts
the lantern so you can see its face properly.

If you look, :choice{to="17"}.

If you run for the front door, :choice{to="18"}.

## 16

The garden goes dark, and the lantern at the cellar door goes out at the same moment, as lamps do
when they are the same lamp.

If you light it again, :choice{to="19"}.

If you leave the house by the front door and keep walking, :choice{to="20"}.

## 17

It is your face, and it is kind, and it is sorry. "Go back up," it says, "and shut the door, and this
time leave the lamp on."

If you do as you are told, :choice{to="19"}.

## 18

The front door opens onto the garden, the shed, and the cellar door standing open at the bottom of
it. Every door in this house does, now.

*Your story ends here.*

## 19

You shut the cellar door, and you bolt it from the kitchen side, and you leave the lamp burning in
the window all night. Nothing comes up.

In the morning there is a green key on the mat, and the shed door is open, and it is beginning again
— but it is morning, and you know one thing you did not know before.

*Your story ends here, for now.*

## 20

You walk until the houses stop. Behind you the lamp comes on in the kitchen window, and you do not
turn round to see who lit it.

*Your story ends here.*
