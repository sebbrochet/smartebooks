# 3. Fouls, cards and what follows

A foul stops play. A card is a separate decision about the player who committed it,
and the two do not always go together: most fouls are given with no card at all.

> 📌 **Key concept**: the punishment fits what the player did, not what happened to
> come of it. A reckless tackle that misses is still reckless.

## When it is a foul

Careless, reckless, or using excessive force — those three words do most of the work
in the laws. Careless is a free kick. Reckless is a caution. Excessive force is a
sending-off, whether or not anyone was hurt.

## Test what you know

:::quiz{id="ch3-cards"}

### A player is shown a second yellow card. What happens?

- [ ] Nothing more — yellows do not add up
- [x] They are sent off, and the team plays on with ten
- [ ] They are substituted

> Explanation: Two cautions in the same match mean a dismissal, and the team is not allowed to replace the player.

:::

## Cards, and what they cost

A caution follows the player beyond the match: collect enough across a season and a
suspension follows. A dismissal costs the team immediately, because nobody comes on
to replace the player who left.

:::flashcard{id="ch3-card-card"}
**Front:** What is the difference between a direct and an indirect free kick?

**Back:** You can score straight from a direct free kick. From an indirect one, someone else must touch the ball first.
:::
