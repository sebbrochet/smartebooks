# 1. The basics

Twenty-two players, one ball, and one person who decides what just happened. This
short course is about that last part: what the referee is watching for, what the
signals mean, and how the close calls are settled.

> 📌 **Key concept**: the laws are written to be applied by one person in real time,
> from one angle, at full speed. Almost everything odd about them follows from that.

## What the referee is for

A referee is not a rule book with legs. The laws ask for *judgement* — whether a
challenge was careless, whether a handball was deliberate, whether stopping play
would help the team that was just fouled. Two referees can see the same tackle and
give different decisions, and both can be right.

## Where the laws come from

The laws are not the property of any league. They are kept by a single body that
meets once a year to agree what changes, which is why the game you play in a park
is the game you watch on television — same seventeen laws, same everywhere.
